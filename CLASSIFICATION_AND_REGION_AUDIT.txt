INDIA'S CULINARY MEMORY ARCHIVE — CLASSIFICATION GUIDE

Every food entry now has three classification fields:
- recordType: the broad kind of food record.
- foodForm: the more specific culinary form.
- definition: a short explanation of why the label fits the entry.

The controlled recordType vocabulary used in this project is:

1. Prepared dish
   A cooked or assembled savoury food preparation, such as a curry, stew,
   rice preparation or kebab.

2. Meal / food combination
   A customary pairing or combination of food components commonly eaten as
   a meal, rather than a single preparation.

3. Ceremonial meal / feast
   A structured multi-dish meal associated with customary, celebratory or
   ceremonial serving practices.

4. Snack / street food
   A ready-to-eat savoury preparation commonly consumed as a snack or sold
   as street food.

5. Sweet / confectionery
   A sweet preparation, dessert or confection.

6. Beverage
   A drink, whether brewed, infused or fermented.

7. Bread / staple
   A grain-based bread or staple food commonly served as a base or
   accompaniment.

8. Fermented / preserved food
   A food whose defining character comes from fermentation, drying or other
   preservation, often used as an ingredient or accompaniment.

9. Condiment / accompaniment
   A preparation primarily served alongside another food, such as a chutney
   or dried-fish mixture.

10. Traditional edible fruit
    A naturally produced edible fruit consumed as food. Hoka Fruit is
    classified here because it is not a cooked dish or recipe. Its inclusion
    documents a regional food resource and local consumption practice.

The classes are editorial catalogue categories, not claims that every
community uses the same terminology. More than one culinary role may exist;
recordType identifies the principal role used for this archive entry.

REGION TYPE AUDIT
The archive contains 28 States and 8 Union Territories (36 regions total).
Goa is a State today. It became the 25th State of the Indian Union on
30 May 1987. It was previously part of the Union Territory of Goa, Daman
and Diu, which explains why historical sources may refer to a different
status. The present-day Union Territory in this archive is Dadra and Nagar
Haveli and Daman and Diu.

HOKA FRUIT — Diu
The Diu–Daman tourism brochure describes Hoka palms as branching palms
native to the Nile valley in Egypt and Sudan and says Portuguese traders
brought them to Diu. It describes the ripe fruit as deep red, sweet-and-sour,
with hard fibrous flesh, and popular among local residents. The brochure
does not provide a recipe or an exact introduction date. The page therefore
uses "Traditional edible fruit" rather than "Prepared dish" and presents
food characteristics and consumption instead of ingredients and method.
